Making a CV that applicant tracking systems can read

At large companies and on job sites, your CV is often read first by an applicant tracking system, or ATS. It extracts the text, files it into fields and compares it with the job ad. If your CV is misread, it can be rejected before a human sees it.

1. A simple layout

A single column reads best. Avoid tables, text boxes and text inside images: many systems read them out of order or skip them. The Classic and Elegant templates on CV.express are designed for this: all text is real text, in the right order.

2. Standard section headings

The software recognises sections by their heading. Use the expected names: "Work experience", "Education", "Skills", "Languages". An original heading such as "My journey" may not be understood.

3. Keywords from the job ad

Reread the job ad and spot the important words: job title, software, techniques, required qualifications. Reuse the ones that genuinely match your profile, in the same words. If the ad says "accounts payable", write "accounts payable", not just "accounting".

4. Clear dates

Give a month and a year for each job ("Mar 2022 – Present"). The software then works out your years of experience, which is often used for screening.

5. A PDF made of real text

Send a PDF created from text, not a photo or scan of your CV: the software would get nothing from it. To check, open your PDF and try to select a sentence: if you can copy it, the software can read it. CV.express PDFs are always made of real text.

The Classic template is the safest for tracking systems. You can also import your old CV to see how it is read.
