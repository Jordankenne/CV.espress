Writing a nurse CV

In healthcare, recruiters check three things first: your qualification, the wards you have worked on, and what you can actually do. A good nurse CV shows them in under ten seconds of reading.

1. Qualification first, with your registration number

State your nursing degree or diploma, the school, the year you qualified and, if you have one, your registration number with the nursing council or professional register. Add any specialisations: paediatrics, theatre, anaesthesia, public health.

2. One job = one ward

For each position, give the ward or department (A&E, internal medicine, paediatrics, maternity, elderly care…), the type of facility (hospital, clinic, health centre, home care) and the number of beds or patients. "Staff nurse, internal medicine, 28 beds, Example University Hospital" speaks to the recruiter immediately.

Just starting out? Your clinical placements count as experience: list them with the ward and the length.

3. Precise clinical skills

  • Care: complex dressings, cannulation, blood sampling, post-operative monitoring, pain management.
  • Emergencies: first aid, resuscitation, triage.
  • Organisation: electronic care records, ward pharmacy, mentoring students.

Group them by category in the Skills section so the recruiter finds them at a glance.

4. People skills, proven by facts

Listening, calm, teamwork: everyone writes them. Show them in your tasks instead: "Supporting families in palliative care", "Trained 6 healthcare assistants in hygiene protocols".

5. Availability and languages

Say whether you accept night shifts, weekends or 12-hour shifts. Languages are a real asset with patients: give your level in each, including local languages.
