Writing a CV for Canada and Quebec

A Canadian CV does not look like an African or French one. The recruiter wants to see in a few seconds what you can do and what you have achieved, with nothing that could lead them to judge anything other than your skills.

1. Remove the photo and personal details

In Canada, human rights laws forbid choosing a candidate based on age, origin, religion or family status. For this reason, recruiters prefer not to receive this information. So include no photo, no date of birth, no nationality and no marital status. Your name, city, phone number with the country code, e-mail address and, if you have one, your LinkedIn profile are enough.

2. Two pages at most, most recent first

One page for a first job, two pages at most after that. List your experience from the most recent to the oldest. In English-speaking Canada the document is often called a “résumé”; in Quebec, a “CV”.

3. Achievements, not duties

Under each job, write three to five lines that start with an action verb and show a result: “Reduced late customer payments by 30%” rather than “Responsible for customer follow-up”. Figures (budget, team size, volumes) make your experience abroad concrete for a Canadian recruiter.

4. State your work status

If you are abroad or immigrating, one line under your title reassures the recruiter: “Permanent resident”, “Open work permit valid until March 2027” or “Arriving in Toronto in September”. Only state what is true and already granted.

5. Make your qualifications understandable

A Canadian recruiter may not know your diploma. Add the equivalent in brackets, and mention the official evaluation if you have it: an Educational Credential Assessment (for example by WES) in most provinces, or the Comparative Evaluation for Studies Done Outside Québec in Quebec.

6. Write in the language of the job advert

In Quebec, French is the working language: write your CV in French. Elsewhere, English is the rule. If the advert asks for both languages, give your level in each, and prepare a version of your CV in each language.

7. Keep references separate

Do not put your referees' contact details on the CV. Prepare them on a separate sheet (name, position, phone, how they know you), with their consent, and hand them over when asked.
