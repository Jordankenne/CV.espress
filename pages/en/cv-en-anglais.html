Writing your CV in English

A CV in English is not a word-for-word translation of your CV in your own language. Section headings, verbs and even the way dates are written change. Here is what matters for an English-speaking recruiter to read you easily.

1. The right section headings

  • Profile (or Summary): your opening statement;
  • Work Experience: your jobs;
  • Education: your qualifications;
  • Skills and Languages;
  • Interests: one line is enough.

In the United Kingdom it is called a “CV”; in the United States and English-speaking Canada a “résumé”, often shorter (one page).

2. Action verbs, without “I”

Each line starts with a verb, in the past tense for a previous job and the present tense for your current one, and without “I”: “Managed a team of 5”, “Increased sales by 20%”, “Prepared monthly VAT returns”. Avoid “Responsible for…”: it describes a duty, not a result.

3. Dates and figures

Write months as abbreviated words: “Mar 2022 – Present” rather than “03/2022”, which is read differently in the United States. In numbers, the decimal separator is a point: “2.5 million”.

4. Do not translate your diploma, explain it

Keep the original name and add an understandable equivalent: “Licence en comptabilité (equivalent to a Bachelor's degree in Accounting)”, “Laurea triennale (Bachelor's degree, 3 years)”. A rough translation can suggest a different level.

5. Your language level, honestly

Use the European levels (A1 to C2) or simple words: Native, Fluent, Intermediate, Basic. The recruiter will check during the interview: an overstated level shows within a minute.

6. Photo and personal details

In the United Kingdom, the United States and Canada, CVs include no photo, age or marital status. For an English-language job in another country, follow that country's customs.

7. Get it proofread

A spelling mistake in a CV is costly. Choose American or British spelling and keep it throughout (organize or organise), then have your CV read by someone with good English.

Choose “English” as the document language: section headings and dates are written in English. In “My space”, “Copy in…” makes a copy of your CV to translate, without touching the original.
