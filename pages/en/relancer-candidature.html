Following up on an application without being a nuisance

A recruiter often receives dozens of applications for a single job. A short, polite follow-up brings your name back at the right time and shows that you really want the job. It will never cost you a place.

1. When to follow up

  • After sending your CV: 7 to 10 working days, unless the advert gives a reply date; then wait for that date.
  • After an interview: a short thank-you message the same day or the next, then a follow-up if you have no news by the date you were given (or a week later).
  • No more than two follow-ups: with no answer after the second, move on to other offers.

2. By e-mail, replying to your first message

Reply to the e-mail in which you sent your CV: the recruiter finds your application at a glance. Keep the same subject, starting with “Follow-up:”.

3. A sample message

Dear Hiring Manager,

On 3 March I sent you my application for the Accounting Assistant position (reference AC-12). I am still very interested in this role: my experience with VAT returns and bank reconciliations matches your needs.

I am available for an interview at your convenience, and I attach my CV again.

Kind regards,

Four or five lines are enough: the date and the position, your interest, one argument, your availability. No reproach, no “I still have not heard back”.

4. What about the phone?

In a small company, a brief call works well: introduce yourself, mention the position and the date you applied, and ask how the recruitment is going. Prepare two sentences about your profile: you may be asked a question straight away.

5. After a rejection

Reply anyway: thank them, say you remain interested in the company, and ask in one sentence what you were missing. The advice helps with your next application, and a courteous answer leaves a good impression.
