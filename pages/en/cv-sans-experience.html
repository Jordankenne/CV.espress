Writing a CV when you have no experience

Everyone started with an almost empty CV. A recruiter hiring an intern or a beginner is not looking for ten years of career: they are looking for someone reliable who learns fast. Your CV has to show it.

1. Start with an opening line

Two lines at the top of the CV: who you are, what you are looking for, from when. For example: “Second-year accounting student looking for a three-month internship starting in June.”

2. Put your education first

Qualification in progress or obtained, institution, year. Add what sets you apart: a distinction, a ranking, the subjects related to the job, the topic of your dissertation.

3. Anything that looks like work counts

  • Internships, even short ones, even observation placements.
  • School projects: a presentation, a case study, an application, a field survey.
  • Odd jobs: sales, private tutoring, helping in the family shop. They prove that you are reliable.
  • Volunteering and community life: an association's committee, organising an event, supervising young people.

For each one, write what you did, with a figure if possible: “Ran the till, 50 customers a day”, “Organised a tournament for 12 teams”.

4. Detail your skills

Computer tools (Word, Excel, software specific to your field), languages spoken with your level, driving licence. Be precise: “Excel: pivot tables” is better than “Good computer skills”.

5. Stay on one page, and tell the truth

One page is more than enough. Do not make anything up: an invented experience is spotted within two questions at the interview. A short, true CV is better than a full, false one.
